VQE on NISQ Devices
A Comparative Study of the H2 Molecule and the Ising Model

Author: Athleity
Date: 2025


ABSTRACT

- This report presents a complete implementation of the Variational Quantum Eigensolver (VQE) applied to two quantum systems of comparable size.
- The hydrogen molecule represents the quantum chemistry case, while the transverse field Ising model represents the condensed matter case.
- The work proceeds in three stages: ideal noiseless simulation, noisy simulation with depolarizing errors, and error mitigation.
- Ideal runs establish the best accuracy that VQE can reach with the chosen hardware-efficient ansatz, giving a lower bound on algorithmic error.
- Noisy runs quantify how much realistic hardware errors degrade the accuracy of the computed ground-state energy on both systems.
- Zero-Noise Extrapolation recovers most of the lost accuracy, while Measurement Error Mitigation proved ineffective in the form used here.


1. INTRODUCTION

- Quantum computers promise to simulate quantum physical systems with an efficiency that scales far better than classical simulation methods.
- Current devices operate in the NISQ era, characterized by limited qubit counts, short coherence times, and high gate and readout error rates.
- VQE was designed specifically for this restricted regime by combining a shallow parameterized circuit with a classical optimization loop.
- The circuit, called the ansatz, is prepared on the quantum processor, and the expectation value of the target Hamiltonian is measured repeatedly.
- A classical optimizer then adjusts the parameters and the loop repeats until the energy converges to a minimum.
- The variational principle guarantees that the computed energy is always an upper bound on the true ground-state energy of the system.
- The central question of this project is whether VQE can deliver useful accuracy on realistic noisy hardware, and whether error mitigation can close the remaining gap to chemical accuracy.


2. SYSTEMS AND HAMILTONIANS

- The hydrogen molecule was modeled at its equilibrium bond length of 0.735 Angstroms, where the two nuclei are energetically most stable.
- Its Hamiltonian was reduced to four qubits with seventeen Pauli terms containing both Z-type and X/Y correlation contributions.
- Classical exact diagonalization gives the H2 ground-state energy as -2.083700 Hartree, which serves as the reference for all error calculations.
- The transverse field Ising model was configured with four qubits on a periodic ring, coupling strength J = 1.0, and transverse field h = 0.5.
- Its Hamiltonian contains only eight Pauli terms, making it structurally simpler than H2 and useful as a control for comparison.
- Classical diagonalization gives the Ising ground-state energy as -4.271558, which is the reference value for all reported errors.


3. METHODOLOGY

- A hardware-efficient ansatz was used with two layers of RY rotations and a ring of CNOT entanglers, giving eight trainable parameters per system.
- The COBYLA optimizer drove the classical parameter updates, running up to one hundred iterations for ideal runs and eighty for noisy runs.
- Initial parameters were drawn uniformly from the interval [0, pi/4] to provide a reasonable starting point for optimization.
- Ideal runs used exact statevector evaluation, eliminating shot noise and giving the true VQE accuracy for the chosen ansatz.
- Noisy runs used the Aer simulator with a depolarizing noise model, with one percent error on single-qubit gates and two percent on two-qubit gates.
- Each Pauli term was measured with 4096 shots to reduce statistical sampling noise to an acceptable level.
- Zero-Noise Extrapolation ran the circuit at four noise levels and fitted a line to extrapolate the energy back to zero noise.
- Measurement Error Mitigation calibrated readout errors and applied a simple correction to the measured expectation values.


4. IDEAL SIMULATION RESULTS

- VQE converged to -2.083259 Hartree for H2, giving an absolute error of 0.00044 Hartree or about 0.021 percent relative error.
- This is well below the chemical accuracy threshold of roughly one kilocalorie per mole, indicating that the ansatz is expressive enough for H2.
- VQE converged to -4.241723 for the Ising model, an absolute error of 0.0298 and a relative error of about 0.70 percent.
- The Ising result is slightly less precise than H2 because the ansatz was not tailored to exploit the model's translational symmetry.
- Both systems converged smoothly within the hundred-iteration budget, with H2 completing in 0.51 seconds and Ising in 0.42 seconds.


5. NOISY SIMULATION RESULTS

- The noisy VQE energy for H2 rose to -1.813098 Hartree, giving an absolute error of 0.271 and a relative error of about 12.99 percent.
- This is roughly 613 times worse than the ideal simulation, showing how severely even modest gate errors can degrade VQE accuracy.
- The noisy VQE energy for Ising rose to -3.853027, giving an absolute error of 0.419 and a relative error of about 9.80 percent.
- This is roughly 14 times worse than the ideal case, much less degraded than H2 despite using the same physical noise model.
- The reason for the disparity is that H2 has seventeen Pauli terms compared to eight for Ising, requiring many more basis rotations per run.
- Each extra rotation adds single-qubit gates to the circuit, so H2 accumulates roughly 44 times more error growth than Ising.
- Runtime also grew substantially, with noisy H2 taking about 102 seconds and noisy Ising about 41 seconds due to per-term measurement overhead.


6. ERROR MITIGATION RESULTS

- Zero-Noise Extrapolation reduced the H2 energy error from 0.271 to 0.064, a 4.25-fold improvement, bringing the relative error down to 3.06 percent.
- Zero-Noise Extrapolation reduced the Ising energy error from 0.419 to 0.142, a 2.95-fold improvement, bringing the relative error down to 3.32 percent.
- Both ZNE results still exceeded the chemical accuracy threshold, but came much closer to the exact value than the raw noisy runs.
- Measurement Error Mitigation changed the H2 energy only to -1.812420, essentially identical to the raw noisy value and offering no benefit.
- Measurement Error Mitigation produced -3.834734 for the Ising model, which is marginally worse than the raw noisy energy of -3.853027.
- The reason MEM failed is that the implementation used a single scalar correction factor rather than inverting the full calibration matrix.
- A rigorous matrix-inversion approach would likely recover much of the potential effectiveness of measurement error mitigation.


7. DISCUSSION

- VQE works well in the ideal case, achieving chemical accuracy for H2 and smooth convergence for Ising within a modest iteration budget.
- Noise is the dominant obstacle, with one percent gate errors causing more than ten percent energy error, far above practical chemistry tolerances.
- System-specific sensitivity matters, since H2 degrades roughly forty-four times more than Ising under the same physical noise.
- Error mitigation helps but does not fully solve the problem, since ZNE reduces error by three to four times but falls short of chemical accuracy.


8. CONCLUSIONS

- VQE is a viable approach for finding ground-state energies of small quantum systems in ideal simulation.
- Realistic hardware noise severely degrades accuracy on both systems, limiting practical usefulness without mitigation.
- Zero-Noise Extrapolation recovers about 75 percent of the accuracy lost to noise and is a practical technique for near-term devices.
- Measurement Error Mitigation was ineffective in the simple form used here, and needs a more rigorous implementation to provide benefit.
- Combining mitigation with circuit optimization or better ansatz design would likely close the remaining gap to chemical accuracy.


9. LIMITATIONS

- Only four-qubit systems were studied, which limits generalizability to larger molecules or longer spin chains.
- The noise model is depolarizing and therefore somewhat idealized, omitting leakage, crosstalk, and coherent error channels.
- The Measurement Error Mitigation step was deliberately kept simple, using a scalar correction instead of a full calibration matrix.
- No statistical error bars are reported, since ideal runs use exact statevector evaluation and noisy runs use a fixed random seed by convention.
- The ansatz was hardware-efficient but not tailored to either system, which may have limited the achievable accuracy.


10. FUTURE WORK

- Test the same circuits on real IBM Quantum hardware to obtain a more realistic noise profile than the depolarizing model used here.
- Scale up to larger molecules such as LiH and H2O to see whether the observed trends continue as system size grows.
- Explore adaptive ansatze like ADAPT-VQE, which builds the circuit iteratively and may reduce depth for a given accuracy.
- Improve Measurement Error Mitigation by inverting the full calibration matrix, which should recover much of its potential effectiveness.
- Compare VQE against other NISQ algorithms such as QAOA and SSVQE on the same systems to identify the best algorithm per problem class.
